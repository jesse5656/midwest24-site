## Shared color family, distinct brands

Jesse confirmed that the two brands share colors. Use the documented brand
anchors: navy `#001F4D`, Core blue `#0EA5FF`, bright-blue accent `#22B8FF`, white
`#FFFFFF`. For Core shields, match the approved shaded artwork, not flat swatches:
royal-blue/navy interior, upper-left cyan illumination, subtle central ridge,
dimensional navy rim and smoothly curved white inset. Preserve the lighting and
white dimensional pictograms. Do not trace the shield with coarse polygon
segments or replace its field with solid turquoise.

Keep Midwest24 Core and MIDWESTGuard names, wordmarks and logo geometry distinct.
Shared colors do not authorize replacing MIDWESTGuard's logo with a Core app
shield. Existing MIDWESTGuard CSS uses other blue values; do not silently change
website styles as part of artwork filing or claim those values already match
the shared anchors. Record a palette migration separately when requested.

## Delivery

Preserve approved originals. Keep source, reference, export and live asset
states distinct. The approved sheet is raster approval evidence, not a vector
master or fifteen finished transparent icon files. Use the existing
`midwest24-core-icon-design` skill for detailed Core icon work.

For new artwork, review it against the actual approved reference enlarged and
at its intended display size. For app icons, show the complete family with
distinct pictograms and consistent shield proportions, light, colors and edges.
Archive provenance and update the branding README with actual rebuild/reuse
instructions. Do not declare a generator compliant without visually checking
its output; the historical `build_launcher_icons.py` flat-cyan output is not
approved by the October 29 sheet.

Filing or approval alone does not authorize deployment. When publication is in
scope, verify the actual served files and application mapping, not just local
previews. Report saved, committed/pushed and deployed states separately.
